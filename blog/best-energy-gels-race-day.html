Best Energy Gels for Race Day: A Runner's Guide (2026)

The right gel at the right mile can make or break your race. Here's everything you need to know about fueling on the course.

10 min read February 10, 2026

You've put in the training miles. You've dialed your pace. But if you hit mile 18 of a marathon with nothing left in the tank, none of that matters. Race-day fueling is one of the most important — and most overlooked — parts of preparation.

Energy gels are the simplest, most portable way to keep your glycogen stores topped off during a race. But with dozens of options on the market, picking the right one can feel overwhelming. This guide breaks down the top gels for race day, when to take them, and how many you actually need.

Why You Need Gels on Race Day

Your body stores roughly 90 minutes of glycogen — the fuel your muscles burn during sustained effort. Once those stores run out, you hit "the wall." Energy gels deliver fast-absorbing carbohydrates (typically 20–45g per packet) to delay or prevent that crash.

For races lasting longer than 60–75 minutes, gels aren't optional — they're essential. Even for shorter races like a fast 10K, a single gel 15 minutes before the gun can give you a noticeable boost.

How Many Gels Do You Need? A Distance Guide

One of the most common race-day mistakes is carrying too few gels — or forgetting them entirely. Here's a quick reference:

Race Distance Estimated Time Gels Needed When to Take
5K 15–35 min 0–1 Optional: 1 gel 15 min before start
10K 35–70 min 0–1 1 gel 15 min before start if over 50 min
Half Marathon 1:20–2:30 2–3 1 before start, then every 45 min
Marathon 2:45–5:30+ 4–6 1 before start, then every 30–45 min
Ultra (50K+) 5+ hours 8+ Every 30–45 min, mix with real food

Race-Day Gel Timing Strategy

Having the right gels is only half the equation. Taking them at the right time is just as important.

Before the Race

Take one gel about 15 minutes before the start. This tops off your glycogen stores without sitting heavy in your stomach. Avoid taking one right at the gun — you want it absorbed before you start running hard.

During the Race

Set a fueling interval of every 30–45 minutes. The exact timing depends on your pace and how your stomach handles fuel at effort. Most runners find 45-minute intervals work well for half marathons, while marathon runners benefit from tighter 30-minute windows.

Always take gels with water, not sports drink. Combining a gel with sports drink creates an overly concentrated sugar solution that can cause cramping and nausea. If you know a water station is coming up at mile 6, plan to take your gel at mile 5.5.

The Golden Rule

Never try a new gel on race day. Always test your fuel during training runs. What tastes great at home can turn your stomach at race pace. Use your long runs to rehearse your exact fueling plan — same gels, same timing, same water intake.

How to Carry Gels During a Race

Where you stash your gels matters more than you think. Fumbling with a gel at pace wastes time and energy.

  • Shorts pocket: Most running shorts have a small back pocket or side pocket. Good for 1–2 gels.
  • Race belt / FlipBelt: The best option for 3+ gels. Distributes weight evenly and keeps everything accessible.
  • Pin to your bib: Some runners use safety pins to attach gel packets to their race bib or shorts waistband.
  • Handheld bottle with pocket: Many handheld bottles have a small zippered pouch — perfect for a couple of gels.

Whatever method you choose, practice it in training. You should be able to open and consume a gel with one hand without breaking stride.

Pack Your Race Bag the Night Before

The worst feeling is standing in a starting corral and realizing your gels are sitting on the kitchen counter. Add your gels to your race bag checklist the night before — along with your bib, timing chip, and everything else you need.
